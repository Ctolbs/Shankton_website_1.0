Surfing Playa Gigante

The breaks, the seasons, and how to plan a surf trip on Nicaragua's Emerald Coast — from mellow beach breaks to Popoyo.

Playa Gigante sits in the Tola district of Rivas — the heart of one of Central America's most consistent stretches of surf coast. Warm water year-round, offshore winds most mornings, and a spread of breaks within a short drive means you can surf every day of a trip and rarely repeat yourself. Here's how it works.

Why surfers come here

Nicaragua's Pacific coast catches swell nearly all year, and the local geography does something special: the big inland lake, Lake Nicaragua, funnels near-constant offshore winds across this stretch, grooming the waves clean most mornings. Add uncrowded lineups compared to Costa Rica next door, and you have a surf destination that still feels like a secret. Playa Gigante puts you right in the middle of it.

The breaks near Playa Gigante

You've got everything from forgiving beach breaks to serious reef and point setups within roughly 20–40 minutes. A few that shape most trips:

Popoyo

The region's marquee wave and one of the most consistent breaks in Central America — a mix of a main reef break and an outer reef that handles size. About 20–30 minutes from Gigante. Best for confident intermediate-to-advanced surfers, though there are mellower zones nearby.

Manzanillo

A more forgiving, playful break closer to the beginner-to-intermediate range, roughly 20 minutes away. A good place to warm up, log some waves, or take a lesson before stepping up to Popoyo.

Colorados, Panga Drops & the outer breaks

The Tola coast is dotted with reef and point breaks — Colorados and Panga Drops among the better known — that reward a bit of local knowledge on tides and swell direction. This is where having someone who knows the coast pays off.

Right out front

Redonda Bay, where Shankton Tower sits above the water, and the beach break at Gigante itself give you options without going anywhere — ideal for a dawn session or the kids learning while the crew regroups.

When to go: the surf seasons

  • Peak swell — April to October: The Southern Hemisphere swell season brings the most size and consistency. If you want power, this is it.
  • November: A local favorite — still plenty of swell as the dry season arrives, with beautiful conditions.
  • October — the sweet spot: Same warm water and swell as peak season, but with fewer crowds and lower rates. Book early; people are catching on.
  • Dry season — December to April: Cleaner, sunnier days and reliable offshores; swell is smaller on average but the trade-off is postcard conditions.

Water is boardshorts-warm all year — no wetsuit required.

Skill levels & lessons

This coast works for everyone. Beginners can start on the mellow beach and Manzanillo-style breaks with a local instructor; intermediates get a playground of options; advanced surfers have Popoyo and the outer reefs on the days they turn on. Our GM Nemer matches you to the right break for your level and the day's conditions — and arranges lessons, boards, and shuttles so you're not guessing.

  • Boards & gear: Bring your quiver or arrange rentals locally; ask us ahead of time.

  • Crews: Traveling with a group? Shankton Tower sleeps 12 with a bunk room for the kids — built for surf trips. Prefer something more intimate with reef and snorkeling off your own platform? Shankton Harbour has its own sea shelf.
