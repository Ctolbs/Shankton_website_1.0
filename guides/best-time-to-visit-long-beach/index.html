Best time to visit Long Beach

Sun almost every day — but the season still matters. Here's when to come to Belmont Shore, month by month.

Long Beach has a mild Mediterranean climate and is one of the sunniest spots in the country — there's genuinely no bad month to come. What changes through the year is the water temperature, the crowds, the rates, and one quirky local weather pattern worth planning around. Here's how the seasons shake out in Belmont Shore.

The short answer

If you want the single best window: September and October. The summer crowds thin, the marine layer is gone, the skies are clear, and the ocean is at its warmest all year. For the warmest, liveliest beach scene, come in summer; for quiet and value, winter is lovely and cheap.

Long Beach through the seasons

SeasonMonthsWhat to expect
Summer Jun – Aug Warm, dry, and sunny; the warmest beach days and liveliest 2nd Street evenings. Busiest and priciest, and street parking is tight on weekends. Mornings early summer can start gray (see June Gloom).
Fall Sep – Nov Arguably the best season: warm water, clear skies, thinner crowds, and occasional hot, crystal-clear Santa Ana days. Rates ease after Labor Day.
Winter Dec – Feb Mild and quiet — highs around 65°F, the lowest rates of the year, and gray-whale season offshore. Most of the region's limited rain falls now; pack a light layer.
Spring Mar – May Warming, green, and pleasant, with fewer crowds than summer. Late spring can bring the first of the marine-layer mornings.

The "June Gloom" heads-up

One honest local quirk: from late May through June, a low marine layer often keeps mornings gray and cool along the coast — locals call it "May Gray" and "June Gloom." It's not rain, and it typically burns off to sunshine by midday or early afternoon. If you visit in that window, just plan your beach time for the afternoon and you'll barely notice it. By July the skies are reliably clear.

Best time for warm water and swimming

The Pacific here is bracing in winter and spring (mid-to-upper 50s°F) and warmest from late summer into early fall, reaching roughly 68–70°F. If you want easy, warm swimming, the protected Alamitos Bay side is calmer and feels warmer than the open ocean — one reason it's so good for families. Both Shankton Peninsula suites include beach chairs and an umbrella, so you can just grab and go.

Best time for sunshine and fewer crowds

September and October are the insider's pick. The marine layer is long gone, the water is still warm from summer, the 2nd Street crowds have thinned after Labor Day, and rates come down. Autumn also brings the occasional Santa Ana — offshore winds that deliver hot, brilliantly clear beach days. It's Long Beach at its best.

Best time for value and quiet

Winter (December–February) is the calm, affordable season. Days are mild and often sunny, the neighborhood is peaceful, and nightly rates are at their lowest. It's also whale-watching season — gray whales migrate past the coast roughly December through April, and boats from Long Beach spot dolphins year-round and blue whales offshore in summer. Pack a light layer for cooler evenings and the odd winter shower.

How's the weather, really?

Mild and sunny, nearly always. Summer highs sit in the mid-70s to low-80s°F (24–28°C) and winter highs hover around 65°F (18°C), with cool, comfortable nights year-round. The coast rarely sees extremes — the hottest days usually arrive with autumn Santa Ana winds, and genuine rain is confined to a handful of winter days.

So — when should you go?

  • Best overall: September–October — warm water, clear skies, fewer crowds, better rates.
  • Warmest beach days and buzz: summer (June–August); book early and plan on tight weekend parking.
  • Quiet and best value: winter (December–February).
  • Whale watching: December–April for the gray-whale migration.
  • Heads-up: late May–June can start gray in the mornings — afternoons are sunny.

A few practical notes

  • Parking is the one catch, and it's tightest on summer weekends — each Shankton Peninsula unit includes a dedicated off-street space, which is rare in Belmont Shore.
  • Pack layers. Even in summer, evenings and gray mornings run cool by the water.
  • Surfers: you'll want a wetsuit most of the year; water only gets boardshorts-warm for a few late-summer weeks.

Common questions

What is the best time to visit Long Beach?

September and October are the local sweet spot: the summer crowds thin out, the marine layer is gone, skies are clear, and the ocean is at its warmest. For the warmest, liveliest beach days choose summer (June–August); for quiet and value, come in winter.

What is June Gloom in Long Beach?

June Gloom (and "May Gray") is a low marine layer that keeps mornings gray and cool along the coast from late May through June. It usually burns off to sunshine by midday or early afternoon, so plan beach time for the afternoon during those weeks.

When is the ocean warm enough to swim in Long Beach?

Water is coldest in winter and spring (mid-to-upper 50s°F) and warmest from late summer into early fall, reaching roughly 68–70°F. The protected Alamitos Bay side is calmer and feels warmer than the open Pacific, which is why it's great for families.

Is Long Beach worth visiting in winter?

Yes. Winters are mild and pleasant, with daytime highs around 65°F, far fewer crowds, and lower rates. It's also gray-whale migration season offshore. Pack a light layer for cooler evenings and the occasional winter rain.

When can you see whales near Long Beach?

Gray whales migrate past the coast roughly December through April, and whale-watching boats from Long Beach see dolphins year-round and blue whales offshore in summer. It's an easy half-day trip from Belmont Shore.

How hot does it get in Long Beach?

Long Beach has a mild Mediterranean climate and is famously sunny. Summer highs sit in the mid-70s to low-80s°F (24–28°C) and winter highs around 65°F (18°C). The coast rarely gets extreme; the hottest days usually come with autumn Santa Ana winds.
